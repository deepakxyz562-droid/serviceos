If you've been shopping for software to run your service business, you've run into three terms that all sound like they describe the same thing: **field service management**, **home services software**, and **blue collar CRM**. Vendors use them interchangeably, comparison blogs blur them together, and you're left wondering — are these three different products, or three names for the same thing?

The honest answer: they overlap heavily, but each emphasizes a different angle of the same category. Understanding the distinction helps you choose the right platform and, more importantly, helps you find the content and tools that actually fit your business.

This guide explains what each term means, where they overlap, where they differ, and which one you should be searching for based on your business.

## The short version

- **Field service management (FSM)** is the broadest term — the discipline and software for coordinating any business that sends technicians to customer locations. It applies to residential, commercial, and industrial field service.
- **Home services software** is a subset of FSM specifically targeting businesses serving residential homeowners — plumbers, HVAC, cleaners, landscapers, pest control. The workflows differ (recurring residential maintenance, after-hours emergencies, local SEO review collection).
- **Blue collar CRM** is the customer-management component of either — a CRM built specifically for trade businesses, tracking physical assets, service history, and field tech activity rather than sales pipelines.

In practice, the best platforms — like Fieseros — are all three at once: a field service management platform, focused on home services businesses, with a blue collar CRM at its core.

## Field service management (FSM)

Field service management is the discipline — and the software platform — that coordinates a business's field operations: the technicians, installers, and service crews who travel to customer locations to deliver work.

FSM covers the full lifecycle of every service job:
1. Capturing the lead (phone, web chat, form)
2. Scheduling the appointment
3. Dispatching the right technician with the right skills
4. Routing them efficiently
5. Providing customer and asset history on a mobile device
6. Generating the invoice on-site
7. Collecting payment
8. Following up for a review

FSM applies to dozens of industries — plumbing, HVAC, electrical, landscaping, cleaning, roofing, pest control, handyman, medical equipment servicing, telecom installs, commercial HVAC, and appliance repair. It serves both residential (home services) and commercial field service businesses.

**When to search for "field service management":** when you want the broad category — comparing platforms across residential and commercial, or researching the discipline itself.

## Home services software

Home services software is FSM focused on residential homeowners. The category includes plumbers, HVAC contractors, electricians, landscapers, house cleaners, handymen, pest control, roofers, pool service, window cleaners, garage door installers, solar installers, and pet services.

The residential focus brings specific workflow differences:

- **Recurring residential maintenance** — seasonal HVAC tune-ups, annual water heater flushes, weekly lawn care, monthly house cleaning. Recurring revenue is the highest-margin revenue in home services.
- **After-hours emergencies** — homeowners call at 11pm when the basement floods. A 24/7 AI receptionist captures these leads; commercial B2B doesn't have the same after-hours pattern.
- **Google review collection for local SEO** — residential businesses live and die by Google Maps rankings. Automated review requests after each job are essential.
- **Customer self-booking** — homeowners expect to book online like they book a restaurant. Commercial customers expect a phone call.
- **Lower ticket sizes, higher volume** — home services tend to have more jobs at lower dollar values than commercial field service.

**When to search for "home services software":** when your business serves residential homeowners and you want a platform optimized for those workflows.

## Blue collar CRM

A blue collar CRM is the customer-management component of a field service or home services platform — built specifically for trade businesses, not for sales teams.

Where a generic CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho) tracks leads, deals, and email sequences for office-based sales teams, a blue collar CRM tracks the physical reality of a service business:

- **Customer 360 profiles** — every past job, invoice, payment, signature, photo, and SMS conversation
- **Asset & equipment tracking** — per-property water heaters, furnaces, panels, with install dates and warranty
- **Service history** — what you've done for this customer, when, and by which technician
- **Recurring maintenance contracts** — scheduled and automated
- **Field tech context** — the mobile app that gives technicians customer history before they knock

A blue collar CRM isn't a standalone product for most businesses — it's the customer database that ties together scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, and payments. Fieseros, Jobber, and Housecall Pro all include a blue collar CRM as their core; ServiceTitan has a more complex CRM built for large fleets.

**When to search for "blue collar CRM":** when your primary pain is customer management — scattered history, lost service records, no asset tracking — and you want a CRM that understands trades.

## How they overlap

Here's the Venn diagram:

- **All three** share: customer management, scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, mobile apps, payments.
- **FSM + home services software** share: everything — home services is a subset of FSM.
- **Blue collar CRM + FSM/home services** share: the customer database and service history layer. A blue collar CRM is the heart of any FSM or home services platform.
